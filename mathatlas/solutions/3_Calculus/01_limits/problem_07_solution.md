# Problem Statement

**Exercise 7: Limit at a Point and Canceling a Factor.**

Compute
$$\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-4x+3}{x-3}.$$
Explain why the value of the function at $x=3$ is not needed to compute the limit.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

A limit concerns nearby values, not necessarily the value at the exact point.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** A limit concerns nearby values, not necessarily the value at the exact point.

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** $x^2-4x+3=(x-1)(x-3)$. For $x\ne3$, $\frac{(x-1)(x-3)}{x-3}=x-1$. Thus the limit as $x\to3$ is $2$.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

Although the original fraction is undefined at $x=3$, values on both sides follow the continuous expression $x-1$.
