# Problem Statement

**Exercise 9: One-Sided Limits and an Asymptote.**

Analyze the left-hand and right-hand limits of
$$f(x)=\frac1{x-2}$$
as $x\to2$. Does the two-sided limit exist? Interpret the result on a sketch of the graph and identify the vertical asymptote.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

A vertical asymptote occurs when the function becomes unbounded near a fixed $x$.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** A vertical asymptote occurs when the function becomes unbounded near a fixed $x$.

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** For $x\to2^-$, $x-2<0$ and tends to 0, so $1/(x-2)\to-\infty$. For $x\to2^+$ it tends to $+\infty$. Therefore no two-sided finite or infinite limit exists; $x=2$ is the vertical asymptote.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

Test $x=1.9$: $f(x)=-10$; $x=2.1$: $f(x)=10$. Signs confirm the opposite one-sided behavior.
