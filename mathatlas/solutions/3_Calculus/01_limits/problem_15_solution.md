# Problem Statement

**Exercise 15: Observation Versus Proof.**

Choose one sequence and one function from this set. For the sequence, compute several values for increasingly large $n$. For the function, prepare a table of values with arguments approaching the limit point from the left and right. Sketch the behavior of both objects.

Compare the observation with the analytical result and explain why a finite number of calculations or a sketch does not constitute a proof that the limit exists.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

A table can illustrate a limit, but only algebra or a theorem can establish it.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** A table can illustrate a limit, but only algebra or a theorem can establish it.

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** Choose sequence $a_n=(4n^2-3n+1)/(2n^2+5n-7)$: $a_{10}=371/243\approx1.527$, $a_{100}=39701/20493\approx1.9373$, $a_{1000}=3997001/2004993\approx1.9935$, tending to 2. Choose function $g(x)=(x^2-4x+3)/(x-3)=x-1$ for $x\ne3$. At $x=2.9,2.99,3.01,3.1$, values are $1.9,1.99,2.01,2.1$. The graph is the line $y=x-1$ with a hole at $(3,2)$.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

Algebra proves the sequence limit by dividing by $n^2$ and the function limit by canceling $x-3$. Tables alone sample finitely many inputs and cannot prove all nearby behavior.
