# Problem Statement

**Exercise 6: From a Sequence to a Function — Limit at Infinity.**

Compute
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2-x+1}{5x^2+4x-2}.$$
Compare the method with Exercise 1. Explain what changes when moving from $n$ to a real variable $x$, and what stays the same.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

The same leading-degree rule works for real $x\to\infty$.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** The same leading-degree rule works for real $x\to\infty$.

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** $\frac{3x^2-x+1}{5x^2+4x-2}=\frac{3-1/x+1/x^2}{5+4/x-2/x^2}\to\frac35$.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

The denominator tends to 5 after division, so the quotient limit law applies.
