# Problem Statement

**Exercise 2: Distance as the Length of a Vector.**

The points are $A=(1,2,3)$ and $B=(4,-2,6)$.

1. Construct $\overrightarrow{AB}$.
2. Compute its length.
3. Compute the distance between the points using the coordinate formula.
4. Explain why both calculations give the same result.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

The vector from $A$ to $B$ is $B-A$; its Euclidean norm is the distance.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** The vector from $A$ to $B$ is $B-A$; its Euclidean norm is the distance.

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** $\overrightarrow{AB}=(4-1,-2-2,6-3)=(3,-4,3)$. Length $\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{34}$. The coordinate distance formula gives the same $\sqrt{34}$.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

Both methods square exactly the coordinate differences $(3,-4,3)$, so they agree.
