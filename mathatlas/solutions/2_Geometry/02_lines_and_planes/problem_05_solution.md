# Problem Statement

**Exercise 5: Relative Position of Two Lines.**

Analyze the position of
$$L_1:(x,y,z)=(1,1,0)+t(1,2,1),$$
$$L_2:(x,y,z)=(2,0,1)+s(0,1,-1).$$
If they intersect, give the intersection point. If not, determine whether they are parallel or skew. Justify the classification.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

Lines in 3D may intersect, be parallel, or be skew (neither parallel nor intersecting).

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

**Step 1 — Identify the method.** Lines in 3D may intersect, be parallel, or be skew (neither parallel nor intersecting).

**Step 2 — Calculate and interpret.** Equating coordinates: $1+t=2$ gives $t=1$. Then $1+2t=s$ gives $s=3$. But the $z$ equations demand $t=1-s=-2$, contradiction. Directions $(1,2,1)$ and $(0,1,-1)$ are not proportional. Therefore lines are skew.

## Verification and Consistency Checks

The unique candidate from $x,y$ fails the $z$ equation, and direction vectors are not multiples.
